What is a pale ale? The gateway to craft beer

Published 9 August 2026 · by Katie

If someone asked for one beer to introduce a friend to craft, a good pale ale would be a strong choice. It's hoppy enough to be interesting but balanced enough to be easy, sitting neatly between a mass-market lager and a full-on IPA. Here's what defines it.

Where pale ale sits

A pale ale is a hop-forward beer, usually 4–5.5% ABV, that balances the fruity, citrussy character of hops against a gentle malt sweetness. It's lighter and more sessionable than an IPA, but far more flavourful than a standard lager. The "pale" refers to the malt, which is lighter than in a brown ale or stout, giving a golden-to-amber colour.

Pale ale vs IPA

The two are close cousins. An IPA (India Pale Ale) is essentially a stronger, hoppier pale ale, more bitterness, more aroma, more punch. A pale ale dials all of that back a notch for easier drinking. If an IPA feels like too much, a pale ale is often the sweet spot.

What it tastes like

Expect citrus and light tropical fruit from the hops, balanced by a soft, biscuity malt backbone and a moderate bitterness. American pale ales lean brighter and more citrussy; English pale ales are earthier and more balanced. Either way, it's a refreshing, food-friendly, endlessly drinkable style.

Where to start

Adnams Ghost Ship and countless supermarket pale ales are great, widely available examples. On MyBeerFinder you can find pale ales, see their hops and flavours, and compare prices. If you enjoy one, an IPA is the natural next step up.
