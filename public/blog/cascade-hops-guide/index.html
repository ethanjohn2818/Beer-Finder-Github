Cascade hops: the hop that started American craft beer

Published 15 August 2026 · by Katie

Every craft beer trend has an origin, and a lot of them trace back to one hop: Cascade. It's not the loudest or the most fashionable variety on the shelf, but without it, modern hoppy beer might not exist. If you want to understand craft beer, you start here.

What Cascade tastes like

Cascade is defined by grapefruit and floral notes, with a hint of spice and pine. It's bright and citrussy but gentle, nowhere near as intense as modern tropical hops, which gives it a classic, refreshing character that never goes out of style.

Where it comes from

Released in the US in 1972, Cascade became the signature hop of the American craft movement. When a little Californian brewery called Sierra Nevada built its Pale Ale around Cascade in 1980, it defined a whole style and showed the world what American hops could do. Almost every citrussy beer you drink today owes it a debt.

How it's used

Cascade is a wonderfully versatile hop, used for both bitterness and aroma across pale ales, IPAs and amber ales. It's often paired with Centennial and Amarillo for a classic American citrus stack, and it remains a benchmark against which newer hops are measured.
