Nelson Sauvin hops: white wine in a beer

Published 16 August 2026 · by Katie

Some hops are hard to describe. Nelson Sauvin isn't, once you've had it, you'll recognise it instantly, because it genuinely tastes of white wine. This prized New Zealand hop is one of the most distinctive in the world, and beers that feature it tend to become firm favourites.

What Nelson Sauvin tastes like

The classic descriptors are white wine, gooseberry and grape, specifically, it was named after Sauvignon Blanc, whose character it echoes. There's a fresh, slightly tart, fruity quality that's unlike any American hop. It can also show passionfruit and a subtle dankness depending on how it's used.

Where it comes from

Nelson Sauvin was bred in New Zealand and released in 2000. New Zealand's climate produces hops with a uniquely fruity, wine-like character, and Nelson is the most famous of the bunch. It's also relatively scarce, which is why it often appears in special or higher-priced beers.

How it's used

A little Nelson Sauvin goes a long way, too much can turn harsh, so brewers often blend it with softer hops like Citra to round it out. It's spectacular in hazy IPAs and pale ales where its wine-like fruitiness can shine.
