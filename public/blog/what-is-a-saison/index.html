What is a saison? The farmhouse ale explained

Published 1 August 2026 · by Katie

Saison is one of the most characterful styles in beer, and one of the hardest to pin down. Born on Belgian farms as a summer refresher for seasonal workers, it's now a favourite of craft brewers who prize its dryness, spice and versatility.

What defines a saison

The magic of a saison is in the yeast. Belgian saison yeast throws off distinctive peppery spice and fruity notes as it ferments, and it ferments extremely dry, meaning the beer finishes crisp and light rather than sweet, with a gentle tartness and a lively carbonation. The result is refreshing, complex and deceptively drinkable, usually around 5–6.5%.

A farmhouse history

"Saison" is French for "season". These beers were brewed in the cooler months on Belgian farms and stored to refresh farmhands ("saisonniers") through the summer harvest. That heritage is why they're often called farmhouse ales, and why they pair so naturally with food.

What it tastes like

Expect pepper and spice, orchard and citrus fruit, and a dry, faintly tart, refreshing finish. Some are hopped up like a hoppy pale ale; others kept traditional and rustic. It's one of the best beer styles for the dinner table, its dryness and spice cut through rich food like almost nothing else.
