Christmas stouts: gingerbread, mince pie and pudding in a glass

Published 28 October 2026 · by Katie

If there is one beer style made for Christmas, it is the stout. Dark, rich and roasty, a good stout already tastes of chocolate and coffee. Add a festive twist, gingerbread, mince pie, Christmas pudding, and you have pudding in a glass. This is where a lot of the most exciting seasonal beer is happening right now.

From imperial stout to pastry stout

The base for most Christmas stouts is the imperial stout: a big, strong (often 8% to 12%) version of the style, thick with roasted malt, dark chocolate and dried fruit. It was originally brewed strong to survive the voyage to the Russian imperial court, hence the name. For the difference between stout and its cousin, see our stout versus porter guide.

The modern "pastry stout" takes that rich base and pushes it towards dessert, adding things like lactose (milk sugar) for sweetness and body, plus adjuncts like vanilla, cacao, cinnamon and biscuit. At Christmas, brewers lean right into the theme.

The festive flavours to look for

  • Gingerbread: ginger, cinnamon and brown sugar over a chocolatey stout.
  • Mince pie: dried fruit, citrus peel and spice.
  • Christmas pudding / figgy pudding: raisin, fig, brandy-soaked richness.
  • Chocolate orange: a perennial festive favourite.
  • Salted caramel and biscuit: sweet, indulgent dessert notes.

British brewers like Vault City, Northern Monk and Vocation are especially good at these, and their rich, flavoured stouts show up in supermarkets through the winter.

How to drink them

Treat a big Christmas stout like a dessert, not a session beer. Serve it only lightly chilled, around 10 to 12 degrees, in a small glass or a snifter, and sip slowly. They are gorgeous after dinner in place of pudding, or alongside one. A rich stout with a mince pie or a wedge of Christmas cake is a genuinely brilliant pairing, more on that in our Christmas food pairing guide.

Find one this Christmas

Festive stouts are some of the easiest Christmas beers to find in UK supermarkets. See what is in stock on our Christmas beers page, or use Find Your Flavour and tap "chocolate", "coffee" or "roasted" to dig one out.
