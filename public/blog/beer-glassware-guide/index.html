Does beer glassware actually matter?

Published 13 July 2026 · by Katie

Craft beer bars have a glass for everything, and it can feel like marketing. But glassware genuinely does change your experience of a beer, mostly by controlling aroma, which is most of what we think of as "flavour". Here's the honest, practical version.

Why the glass matters

Two reasons. First, aroma: a glass that narrows towards the top concentrates the hop and malt aromas so you actually smell them as you drink. Second, the pour: pouring from can to glass releases carbonation and aroma that stay trapped if you drink straight from the tin. Even a cheap glass beats no glass.

The shapes that matter

  • The tulip / Teku, narrows at the top to trap aroma; brilliant for hoppy IPAs and strong Belgians.
  • The nonic pint, the classic British pub glass; perfect for bitters, pale ales and lagers.
  • The tall weizen glass, designed for wheat beers and their big fluffy heads.
  • The snifter, small and round for sipping strong stouts and barley wines.

Do you need all of them?

Honestly, no. If you buy one good glass, make it a tulip, it flatters almost everything, from a hazy IPA to a stout. The single biggest upgrade isn't owning ten glasses; it's simply pouring your beer into a glass instead of drinking from the can.

One practical tip

Keep your beer glass clean and rinse out any washing-up liquid residue, grease and detergent kill a beer's head and aroma. A clean glass, a proper pour and the right serving temperature will do more for your beer than anything else.
