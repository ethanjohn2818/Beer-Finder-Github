New Zealand hops: the southern hemisphere's fruit basket

Published 10 August 2026 · by Katie

New Zealand grows some of the most sought-after hops on earth. Isolated at the bottom of the world, its hop varieties have a bright, fruity, slightly wine-like character that's instantly different from their American cousins, and craft brewers can't get enough of them.

What New Zealand hops taste like

As a family, they lean towards fresh citrus, tropical fruit and crisp white-wine notes. The stars include:

  • Motueka, lime, lemon and tropical fruit; clean and zesty.
  • Riwaka, intense grapefruit and passionfruit; prized and scarce.
  • Nelson Sauvin, the famous white-wine-and-gooseberry hop.
  • Nectaron, a newer variety bursting with pineapple and tropical fruit.

Why they taste different

New Zealand's clean air, long sunshine hours and isolation give its hops a distinctive freshness and fruitiness. Because the crop is smaller and shipped a long way, NZ-hopped beers are often a little pricier, but their bright, juicy character makes them worth seeking out.

How they're used

NZ hops are superb in hazy IPAs and pale ales, sometimes on their own to show off their unique character, sometimes blended with American hops for extra fruitiness. If you spot a beer proudly waving the NZ flag, expect something zesty and tropical.
