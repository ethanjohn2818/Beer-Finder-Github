Hofbräu: Munich's royal brewery and its world-famous beer hall

Published 29 September 2026 · by Katie

Hofbräu, properly Hofbräu München, is the Munich brewery with the grandest history and the rowdiest tent. Its name literally means "court brewery", because that is exactly what it began as: the private brewery of the rulers of Bavaria.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

Founded by a duke

Hofbräu was founded in 1589 by Wilhelm V, Duke of Bavaria, who was tired of paying to import beer and decided the royal court should brew its own. For centuries it was the beer of dukes and kings. Even today it is unusual among the six: it is owned by the Free State of Bavaria, making it effectively a state brewery.

The Hofbräuhaus

Hofbräu is inseparable from the Hofbräuhaus, the enormous beer hall in central Munich that grew out of the old court brewery. It is one of the most famous drinking halls in the world, packed every day of the year with locals and visitors under its vaulted, painted ceiling. If you only visit one beer hall in Munich, it is usually this one.

At Oktoberfest

The Hofbräu-Festzelt is the most international of all the Oktoberfest tents, the one where travellers from across the world gather, and famously the liveliest. Its festival beer is a strong golden Festbier in the modern Wiesn style.

What to try

  • Hofbräu Original, a classic Munich Helles, clean and smooth with a soft malt backbone.
  • Hofbräu Münchner Weiße, its wheat beer, if you prefer the banana-and-clove side of Bavaria.
