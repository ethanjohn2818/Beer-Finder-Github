What is a winter warmer? The big beer of the cold months

Published 3 November 2026 · by Katie

When the nights draw in, the beer gets bigger. The winter warmer is exactly what it sounds like: a strong, dark, richly malty ale made to be sipped slowly on a cold evening. It is the classic Christmas beer style, and once you know what to look for, it is one of the most comforting drinks of the year.

What makes a winter warmer

There is no single rulebook, but a winter warmer almost always means:

  • Strength. Usually 6% to 9%, sometimes more. The alcohol itself gives a genuine warming glow.
  • Dark, malty depth. Amber to deep brown, with flavours of caramel, toffee, dried fruit, treacle and bread crust. The malt leads, not the hops.
  • A full body. These are rich, sometimes almost chewy beers, low on fizz and big on comfort.
  • Often, a little spice. Many modern winter warmers add cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger or orange peel for a mince-pie character. Traditional English ones often use no spice at all and let the malt do the work.

Where the style comes from

The winter warmer grew out of the old English "old ale" and strong ale tradition, beers brewed strong so they would keep, and drunk in the depths of winter. In Britain they tend to be malty and restrained. American brewers later took the idea and ran with it, adding festive spices and, in some cases, a big hit of hops. Both count as winter warmers.

How to enjoy one

Do not serve a winter warmer fridge-cold. A little cool of room temperature, around 10 to 12 degrees, lets all that malt complexity come through, the same way you would treat a barley wine. Pour it into a big glass, give it room to breathe, and treat it as a beer to sip rather than gulp. It is brilliant after dinner, especially with a cheeseboard or a slice of Christmas cake.

Winter warmer or old ale or barley wine?

These strong, dark styles blur into each other, and that is fine. Roughly: a winter warmer is the festive, often spiced one; an old ale is the matured, vinous English classic; a barley wine is the strongest and richest of all. If you enjoy one, you will probably enjoy the others.
