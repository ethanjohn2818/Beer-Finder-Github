Red and amber ales: the malty middle ground

Published 25 July 2026 · by Katie

Between the bright hoppiness of a pale ale and the roasty depths of a stout sits a warm, malty middle ground: the red and amber ales. They don't get the hype of an IPA, but for balance and easy drinking they're hard to beat.

What defines them

Red and amber ales are malt-forward beers, taking their colour and flavour from crystal and caramel malts. Where a pale ale lets the hops lead, these let the malt lead, giving flavours of caramel, toffee, biscuit and toasted bread, with just enough hop bitterness to keep them from being sweet. Colours range from deep gold to ruby red.

Amber vs red

The line is blurry and largely about colour and emphasis. Amber ales tend to be a touch hoppier and more balanced; red ales (including the Irish red) lean a little sweeter and maltier, sometimes with a faint roasted dryness. Both share that comforting, caramelly character.

American vs Irish vs British

American amber/red ales add a citrussy hop kick on top of the malt. Irish reds are smooth, malty and easy. British amber and best bitters bring earthy English hops into the mix. All three are brilliant, food-friendly, everyday beers.
