How to read a craft beer can

Published 22 August 2026 · by Katie

A modern craft beer can is covered in numbers, names and buzzwords. Learn to read them and you can tell, at a glance in the shop, roughly what a beer will taste like, no gamble required. Here's the shorthand.

ABV, how strong it is

ABV (alcohol by volume) is the percentage of alcohol. As a rough guide: under 4% is a light session beer, 4–5.5% is standard, 6–8% is a strong IPA or imperial territory, and anything into double figures is a sipper. Higher ABV usually means a fuller, sweeter, more warming beer, useful to know before you buy a four-pack.

IBU, how bitter it is (in theory)

IBU stands for International Bitterness Units. Low numbers (10–25) mean gentle; high numbers (60+) mean assertively bitter. Treat it as a loose hint, though, a hazy IPA and a West Coast IPA can share an IBU number and taste completely different, because bitterness you perceive depends on sweetness and body too.

The hop names

That list of names, Citra, Mosaic, Simcoe, tells you more about flavour than any marketing copy. Citra and Mosaic mean tropical and citrussy; Simcoe and Chinook mean pine and resin; Fuggles and Goldings mean earthy and traditional. This is the single most useful thing on the can, and it's exactly what MyBeerFinder decodes for you.

The style words

"Hazy" and "juicy" mean soft and fruity; "West Coast" means clear and bitter; "session" means lower strength; "double" or "imperial" means stronger. "Pale ale" is a gentler cousin of IPA. These words are a reliable steer once you know the code.

The date, don't skip it

For hoppy beers especially, freshness is everything. Those bright hop aromas fade within months, so check the canning or best-before date and pick the freshest one on the shelf. An old hazy IPA is a shadow of a fresh one.
