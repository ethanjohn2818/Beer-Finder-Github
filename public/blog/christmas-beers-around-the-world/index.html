Christmas beers around the world: a festive tour

Published 4 November 2026 · by Katie

Christmas is the best time of year to be a beer drinker. All over the world, brewers mark the season with something special: bigger, darker, spicier, and stronger than the everyday pint. The traditions differ from country to country, but the idea is the same everywhere, a rich beer to warm you through the longest, coldest nights. Here is a quick tour of how the beer world does Christmas.

Belgium: the Noël ales

Belgium arguably does festive beer best. Its Christmas ales, often labelled "Noël" or "Kerst", are strong, dark and spiced, glowing ruby to deep brown and clocking in anywhere from 8% to 12%. Think dried fruit, caramel, warming alcohol and a whisper of Christmas spice. We go deep on these in our guide to Belgian Christmas ales.

Britain: winter warmers and old ales

The British tradition is the winter warmer: a strong, malty, often spiced dark ale built for a seat by the fire. Alongside it sit the old ales and the occasional cheeky spiced Christmas ale. More in our guide to British Christmas ales.

Germany: the winter bock

Germany answers winter with the bock, a strong, smooth, malty lager. The Doppelbock was literally brewed by monks as "liquid bread" to see them through a fasting winter. See our guide to German winter beers.

Scandinavia: julebryg

In the Nordic countries, the Christmas beer, julebryg or juleøl, is a genuine national event. In Denmark its launch day has its own name and its own party. Read about it in our guide to Scandinavian julebryg.

America: holiday ales and winter warmers

American craft brewers reinvented the Christmas beer with spiced holiday ales and hoppy winter warmers, led by a couple of true classics. See our guide to American Christmas beers.

And the really strong stuff

Some Christmas beers push things to the limit. Samichlaus, brewed only on Saint Nicholas Day (6 December) and aged for months, reaches around 14%. Belgium's Stille Nacht and Bush de Noël are not far behind. These are sipping beers, poured in small measures and savoured slowly.

Bring it home

You do not need to travel to drink the world's Christmas beers. Plenty turn up on UK shelves through December, alongside home-grown winter warmers and festive stouts.
