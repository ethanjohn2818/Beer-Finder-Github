Amarillo hops: orange, floral and easy to love

Published 17 August 2026 · by Katie

Amarillo is one of craft beer's most likeable hops. Where some varieties are loud and divisive, Amarillo is soft, orangey and easy-going, the kind of hop that makes a beer approachable rather than challenging. It's a mainstay of American pale ales for good reason.

What Amarillo tastes like

The signature note is orange, think marmalade and orange blossom, with a gentle floral lift and a touch of lemon. It's fruity without being tropical, and its bitterness is smooth rather than sharp. That balance makes it a brilliant everyday hop.

Where it comes from

Amarillo is an American hop with an unusual story, it was discovered growing wild on a farm rather than deliberately bred, and it's still grown under a single proprietary grower. Its distinctive citrus-floral profile quickly made it a craft favourite.

How it's used

Amarillo shines in pale ales and session IPAs, often paired with Cascade or Centennial for a classic American citrus character, or with Citra and Simcoe in more modern blends. It's soft enough to carry a beer on its own and versatile enough to support almost any hoppy recipe.
