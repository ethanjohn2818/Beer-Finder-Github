Is beer vegan? The finings question explained

Published 9 July 2026 · by Katie

Beer is made from grain, water, hops and yeast, all plant-based, so surely all beer is vegan? Not quite. A traditional ingredient you'll never see on the label means some beers aren't even vegetarian. Here's the story.

The culprit: isinglass

Many traditional beers, especially cask ales, are clarified using isinglass, a substance made from fish swim bladders. It's added to help yeast and haze settle out so the beer pours bright and clear. Almost none of it remains in the finished pint, but because it's an animal product, beers fined with it aren't vegan or vegetarian.

Other finings

Some beers use gelatin (also animal-derived), while many modern brewers use plant-based or synthetic finings like Irish moss (seaweed) or simply filter the beer, both vegan-friendly. Others don't fine at all, which is part of why hazy, unfiltered beers are so often vegan: leaving the haze in means no animal finings needed.

How to tell

The good news is that clarity is a clue and labels are improving. Cloudy craft cans are very frequently vegan; bright, traditional cask ales are the ones to check. Many breweries now state "suitable for vegans" on the can, and sites like Barnivore catalogue which beers are vegan-friendly.

Finding vegan beer

Most modern hazy IPAs and pale ales on the supermarket shelf are vegan, so if you're avoiding animal products, that cloudy can is usually a safe bet, but check the label to be sure. On MyBeerFinder you can browse the styles most likely to be vegan-friendly and compare prices.
