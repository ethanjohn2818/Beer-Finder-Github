Citra hops: the flavour that built modern IPA

Published 29 August 2026 · by Katie

If you've drunk a hazy IPA in the last decade, you've almost certainly drunk Citra. It is the most popular aroma hop in craft brewing, and for good reason: it delivers a huge hit of juicy, tropical flavour with very little of the harsh bitterness older hops were known for. Here's what it actually tastes like, and why it's on the back of so many cans.

What Citra tastes like

The clue is in the name. Citra leans hard into citrus, think grapefruit and lime zest, with a big backing chorus of tropical fruit: mango, passionfruit and lychee. In a pale ale it reads as bright and zesty; in a soft, hazy IPA it turns almost like fruit juice. There's a reason brewers reach for it when they want a beer to taste "juicy" rather than "bitter".

Where it came from

Citra was released in 2008 by the Hop Breeding Company in the United States. It arrived just as brewers were moving away from aggressively bitter beers towards fruit-forward, aroma-driven ones, and it was the perfect hop for the moment. Its high oil content means a little goes a long way, and it plays beautifully with other modern American hops.

Beers that show it off

Plenty of supermarket favourites are Citra-led. Adnams Ghost Ship is a gently Citra-hopped pale ale that's a great, widely available starting point. Rooster's built its Baby-Faced Assassin range entirely around single-hop Citra. Once you know you like it, you can chase it: hops like Mosaic, Nelson Sauvin and Galaxy sit in a similar tropical-and-fruity world and are natural next steps.

Citra on its own vs Citra in a blend

A single-hop Citra beer is a brilliant way to learn the flavour, nothing else is getting in the way. But most beers blend Citra with other hops to add depth: a bit of Simcoe for pine, some Amarillo for orange, Mosaic for a dank, berry edge. So two Citra beers can taste quite different depending on what they're paired with, and how late in the brew the hops go in.
