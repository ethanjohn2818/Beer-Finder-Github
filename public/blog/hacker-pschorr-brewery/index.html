Hacker-Pschorr: the 'Heaven of the Bavarians'

Published 26 September 2026 · by Katie

Hacker-Pschorr is two old Munich brewing names joined into one, and the brewery behind one of the most beautiful tents at Oktoberfest. If you have seen a photo of a festival tent with a pale-blue sky and clouds painted across the ceiling, that is Hacker.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

Two families, one brewery

The Hacker brewery's roots in Munich reach back to 1417. In the late 18th century it passed through marriage to the Pschorr family, and Joseph Pschorr grew it into one of the city's great brewing houses. The two names, Hacker and Pschorr, were formally brought together in the 20th century. Today the brewery is part of the same Munich group as Paulaner, but it is brewed to its own long-standing recipes.

"Himmel der Bayern"

The Hacker-Festzelt is nicknamed "Himmel der Bayern", the Heaven of the Bavarians, thanks to its famous painted sky ceiling and the retractable roof that can open to the real sky above. It is one of the most atmospheric and best-loved tents at the whole festival, known for a lively, musical crowd.

What to try

  • Hacker-Pschorr Münchner Hell, the classic pale Munich lager, soft and malty.
  • Hacker-Pschorr Hefe Weißbier, a well-regarded Bavarian wheat beer, full of banana and clove.
  • Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest-Märzen, a more traditional amber festival beer, when you can find it.

Hacker-Pschorr still bottles a classic amber Oktoberfest Märzen for export, so it is one of the better names to look for if you want the traditional style described in our Oktoberfestbier and Märzen guide.
