Paulaner: the monks' brewery behind Munich's biggest name

Published 1 October 2026 · by Katie

Paulaner is probably the Munich brewery whose name you know best, and the one you are most likely to find on a British shelf. It is one of the largest of the city's breweries and a fixture at Oktoberfest, where its beer flows in the towering Winzerer Fähndl tent.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

Born in a monastery

Paulaner takes its name from the Minim friars of Saint Francis of Paola, who settled in Munich and began brewing around 1634. Monks were allowed to drink beer during Lenten fasting, so they brewed a rich, strong beer to sustain them, "liquid bread". That tradition lives on in Paulaner's Salvator, a dark, powerful Doppelbock that is one of the classic strong lagers of Germany, and the beer that effectively founded the whole Doppelbock style.

What they are known for today

Paulaner brews a full range of Munich classics, but two stand out for most drinkers:

  • Paulaner Hefe-Weißbier, its cloudy wheat beer, is one of the best-known Weissbiers in the world: soft, full of banana and clove, and brilliantly refreshing. It is widely sold in UK supermarkets.
  • Paulaner Münchner Hell, a smooth, gently malty pale lager, is the everyday Munich style, and the closest thing to what you drink in the beer gardens.

Its Oktoberfest beer is a strong golden Festbier, in the modern Wiesn style we explain in our guide to Oktoberfestbier and Märzen.

Find Paulaner in the UK

Paulaner's Weissbier is one of the easiest Bavarian beers to find in Britain. Check the Oktoberfest beers page to see where it is stocked and for the cheapest price, or read our wheat beer guide to understand what gives it that banana-and-clove character. Prost!
