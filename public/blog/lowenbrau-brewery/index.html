Löwenbräu: the roaring lion of Munich beer

Published 27 September 2026 · by Katie

Löwenbräu means "lion's brew", and the lion is everywhere: on the label, over the brewery gates, and roaring from the tower of its Oktoberfest tent. It is one of Munich's best-travelled beer names and one of the six breweries poured at the festival.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

A brewery with deep roots

Records of a Löwenbräu in Munich reach back to the 14th century, and by the 1800s it had grown into one of the city's largest breweries, exporting its lager far beyond Bavaria. The lion emblem became one of the most recognisable in German brewing, and it is said the brewery took the name from an old inn decorated with a painting of a lion.

At Oktoberfest

The Löwenbräu-Festzelt is one of the landmark tents, topped by a tower with a giant lion that periodically lifts its stein and lets out a mechanical roar of "Lö-wen-bräu!" over the crowds. Its festival beer is a strong golden Festbier, like the other tents today.

What to try

  • Löwenbräu Original, a traditional Munich Helles, easy-drinking and gently malty.
  • Löwenbräu Oktoberfestbier, the stronger festival lager, in season.

The Löwenbräu brand is now part of the global AB InBev group, but its Munich beer remains tied to the city and the festival. For the style behind it, see our Oktoberfestbier and Märzen guide.
