Alcohol-free beer has got good: where to start

Published 23 August 2026 · by Katie

A few years ago, ordering an alcohol-free beer meant accepting something watery and a bit sad. Today it's one of the fastest-improving corners of the drinks aisle, and some 0.0% beers are genuinely hard to tell from the real thing. Whether you're driving, pregnant, doing a dry month or just cutting down, here's the state of play.

What "alcohol-free" actually means

In the UK the labels follow rough bands: alcohol-free is 0.05% ABV or below, de-alcoholised up to 0.5%, and low-alcohol up to 1.2%. Many of the best "0.0%" beers actually sit at or just under 0.5%, low enough that you'd need to drink an implausible amount to feel anything.

How they make it

Two main methods. Some brewers remove the alcohol after brewing a normal beer, by gently heating it under vacuum, or filtering it out, keeping much of the flavour. Others stop the alcohol forming in the first place, using special yeasts that produce little or no alcohol, or halting fermentation early. Each has trade-offs, which is why some alcohol-free beers taste fuller than others.

Have they cracked the flavour?

For hoppy styles, largely yes. Alcohol-free pale ales and IPAs lean on aroma hops for all that citrus and tropical fruit, and hops don't need alcohol to taste good, so they translate well. BrewDog's Punk AF and a wave of supermarket own-brand hazy 0.0%s are proof. Rich, malty styles like stout are trickier, but even those are improving fast.

Why it's worth a look

Beyond the obvious, driving, health, pregnancy, alcohol-free beer means you can enjoy the ritual and the flavour of a good beer on a school night without the next-day cost. It's also usually lower in calories. There's very little reason not to keep a few in the fridge.
