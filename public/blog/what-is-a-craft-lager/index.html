Craft lager and pilsner: crisp, clean and underrated

Published 7 August 2026 · by Katie

Lager has an image problem in craft circles — for years it was seen as the boring stuff you drank when there was nothing better. But brewing a great lager is genuinely hard, and a wave of excellent craft lagers, pilsners and helles has made the style cool again.

Ale vs lager: the real difference

The split between the world's two great beer families comes down to yeast. Ales (pale ales, IPAs, stouts) ferment warm with a yeast that works at the top of the tank, giving fruity, expressive flavours. Lagers ferment cold with a yeast that settles at the bottom, then condition for weeks — a slow process that produces a cleaner, crisper, more refined beer.

The lager family

  • Pilsner — crisp and noticeably hoppy, with a spicy, floral bitterness from noble hops. The most flavourful of the pale lagers.
  • Helles — a Bavarian classic; softer and more malt-forward than a pilsner, bready and gently sweet.
  • Craft lager — a broad modern term for well-made pale lagers, sometimes dry-hopped for extra aroma.

Why it's hard to brew well

Lager's clean profile leaves nowhere to hide. There are no big hop or malt flavours to mask a mistake, so any off-note stands out. A brilliant lager is a real test of a brewery's skill — which is exactly why beer geeks have come to respect them.
