Augustiner: Munich's oldest brewery and its local favourite

Published 28 September 2026 · by Katie

Ask someone from Munich which of the six Oktoberfest breweries they actually drink, and the answer is very often Augustiner. It is the oldest brewery in the city, the most fiercely local, and the one that still does things the traditional way.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

Brewing since 1328

Augustiner was founded in 1328 by Augustinian monks next to the cathedral in Munich, which makes it the city's oldest brewery by a wide margin. It later moved out of the monastery but kept its name and its reputation. Unlike most of its rivals it has stayed independent and privately held, and it is famously low-key: very little advertising, no stunts, just beer that Munich trusts.

The local's choice

Augustiner's Lagerbier Hell and its premium Edelstoff are the beers you see on the tables of Munich's beer gardens more than any other. The style is classic Munich Helles: pale gold, soft, clean, gently bready, the kind of beer you can happily drink all afternoon. Nothing flashy, just balance and quality.

At Oktoberfest

The Augustiner-Festhalle is widely considered the most traditional and family-friendly tent at the festival. Crucially, Augustiner is the one brewery that still serves much of its Oktoberfest beer from wooden barrels, called Hirschen, rather than steel kegs, a small thing that locals swear you can taste. If you want the most old-fashioned Oktoberfest experience, this is the tent.

What to try

  • Augustiner Lagerbier Hell, the everyday Munich Helles, soft and endlessly drinkable.
  • Augustiner Edelstoff, a slightly stronger, crisper premium version.

Augustiner is less widely exported than Paulaner or Spaten, so it can be harder to find in Britain, but it is worth seeking out. For more on the style, read our craft lager guide.
