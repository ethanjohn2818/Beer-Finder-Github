Beer and food pairing for beginners

Published 20 August 2026 · by Katie

Wine gets all the credit at the dinner table, but beer is arguably the more versatile food match, it's got sweetness, bitterness, fizz and roast to play with. You don't need to be an expert to pair well, either. A handful of simple rules will get you most of the way.

The three rules that never fail

Forget the jargon and remember these:

  • Match intensity. Delicate food, delicate beer; big bold food, big bold beer. A light lager gets steamrollered by a rich curry; an imperial stout would flatten a green salad.
  • Find a bridge. Look for a flavour the beer and the food share, the caramel in a porter and the char on a steak, the citrus in an IPA and a squeeze of lime on your tacos.
  • Or find a contrast. Sometimes opposites work: a crisp, bitter IPA cuts through greasy fried food; a sweet fruit beer offsets salty cheese.

Pairings that just work

  • Curry & spicy food, a hoppy IPA or a crisp lager. The bitterness and fizz reset your palate between mouthfuls; the fruit stands up to the spice.
  • Burgers & BBQ, a malty amber ale or a porter. The caramel and roast echo the char.
  • Cheese, hugely beer-friendly. Try a Belgian-style ale with soft cheese, a barley wine or stout with a strong blue.
  • Fish & chips, a classic pale ale or pilsner; the bitterness cuts the grease.
  • Chocolate & pudding, an imperial stout or a fruit beer. Dark, sweet and roasty against dark, sweet and roasty.

The palate-cleanser trick

One reason beer beats wine with food is carbonation. Those bubbles, plus a little hop bitterness, physically scrub fat and spice off your tongue between bites, which is why a cold IPA and a hot curry are such a famously good match.
