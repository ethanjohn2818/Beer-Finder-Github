What is a West Coast IPA? The original, done properly

Published 26 August 2026 · by Katie

Before hazy IPAs took over, there was the West Coast IPA, and lately it's made a big comeback. If the hazy style is a fruit smoothie, the West Coast is a cold, clear glass of pine and grapefruit with a satisfying bitter snap. Here's what defines it.

Clear, dry and bitter, on purpose

A West Coast IPA is brewed to be the opposite of hazy. It's crystal clear, has a dry, crisp finish, and wears its bitterness with pride. The malt is kept lean so the hops can shine, and nothing is left sweet or soft. It's a more bracing, refreshing drink than a hazy, and for a lot of long-time IPA fans, it's the real thing.

The flavours

Classic West Coast character means pine, resin and grapefruit, often with orange and a whiff of dank, herbal green. The hops behind it are the old guard of American craft: Simcoe, Centennial, Chinook, Columbus and Cascade, increasingly joined by modern varieties like Citra and Idaho 7 for a citrus lift.

West Coast vs hazy IPA

The quickest way to tell them apart is to hold the glass up. Hazy IPAs are cloudy, soft and juicy with low bitterness; West Coast IPAs are clear, dry and firmly bitter. Same base idea, a hop-forward strong pale ale, taken in two opposite directions. Trying one of each side by side is one of the best ways to work out which camp you're in.

Good ones to look for

Thornbridge's West Side Glory is a gold-medal example on UK shelves, all pine and grapefruit. Plenty of supermarkets now stock their own "West Coast IPA" too. Look for the words "West Coast" on the can, and expect a firmer, drier drink than the juicy stuff next to it.
