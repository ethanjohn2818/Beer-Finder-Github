Belgian beer styles explained: dubbel, tripel and quad

Published 29 July 2026 · by Katie

Long before American hops took over, Belgium was, and still is, one of the great beer nations. Its abbey and Trappist ales are strong, complex and endlessly influential, and understanding them unlocks a whole world of flavour beyond the IPA.

It's all about the yeast

Belgian ales get their signature character not from hops but from expressive yeast, which produces flavours of banana, clove, dark fruit and spice. Many are also brewed with candi sugar, which boosts the strength while keeping the body deceptively light, which is exactly why these strong beers go down so dangerously easily.

The numbered styles

  • Blonde/Blond, golden, gently fruity and spicy, around 6–7%. An easy entry point.
  • Dubbel, dark and malty with raisin and caramel notes, around 6–8%.
  • Tripel, golden but strong (8–9%+), fruity and spicy with a dry, warming finish. A classic.
  • Quadrupel, the biggest, richest and darkest (10%+), full of dried fruit, toffee and warmth. A sipper for cold nights.

Trappist and abbey ales

Some of the world's most revered beers are still brewed by monks in Trappist monasteries. "Abbey" beers are made in the same styles by commercial brewers, often under licence. Either way, expect depth, strength and centuries of tradition in the glass.
