A beginner's guide to gluten-free beer

Published 24 August 2026 · by Katie

For a long time, "gluten-free beer" meant a thin, apologetic drink you settled for. Not any more. Modern gluten-free and gluten-removed beers can be genuinely excellent, and there are more of them on the shelf every year. Here's how they work and how to shop for them.

Why beer usually contains gluten

Most beer is brewed from malted barley, and often wheat too, both of which contain gluten. That's a problem for anyone with coeliac disease or a gluten intolerance, for whom even small amounts matter. The good news is that brewers now have two proven ways around it.

Gluten-free vs gluten-removed

There are two different approaches, and the difference matters:

  • Naturally gluten-free beers are brewed from grains that never contained gluten in the first place, like millet, rice, sorghum or buckwheat. These are safe for coeliacs.
  • Gluten-removed (or "gluten-reduced") beers start as normal barley beer, then use an enzyme to break down the gluten to below the legal 20 parts-per-million threshold. Many taste closer to "normal" beer, but if you're coeliac, check the label carefully and take medical advice, the science on residual fragments is still debated.

Does it taste good now?

Increasingly, yes. BrewDog's Gluten Free Punk IPA, for example, is the same recognisable citrussy pale ale with the gluten removed, most drinkers can't tell the difference. Because the enzyme approach keeps a proper barley base, the hop and malt flavours survive in a way the old sorghum-only beers never managed.

How to find gluten-free beer

One important note

Always read the can itself before drinking, especially if you're coeliac. Labels and recipes change, and only the packaging in your hand is the final word. Our filter and our per-beer allergen guide are here to help you shortlist, not to replace the label.
