Scandinavian julebryg: the Nordic Christmas brew

Published 30 October 2026 · by Katie

Nobody takes Christmas beer quite as seriously as the Scandinavians. In Denmark, Norway and Sweden the festive brew, julebryg or juleøl, is a seasonal institution, and in Denmark its arrival is a genuine national party.

Denmark and J-Dag

The most famous example is Tuborg's Julebryg. Its launch has its own name, J-Dag ("the day of the Christmas beer"), and it falls on the first Friday of November every year. At the stroke of the evening, Tuborg's blue-clad delivery teams fan out to pubs across the country handing out free beer, snow machines and Santa hats included. It is part marketing stunt, part beloved tradition, and it unofficially kicks off the Danish Christmas. Carlsberg and others make their own julebryg too.

What it tastes like

Danish julebryg is usually a strong-ish lager, around 5.6% to 5.8%, a touch darker, maltier and sweeter than the everyday pilsner. It is easy-drinking rather than a sipping monster. Elsewhere in the region the style ranges more widely: Norwegian juleøl and the beers of craft brewers like Nøgne Ø can be much darker and stronger, closer to a spiced winter ale or porter.

An ancient tradition

Christmas brewing in the north is genuinely old. The Norse midwinter feast was called "jól" (the root of our word "Yule"), and brewing and drinking ale for it was a serious business. Under old Norwegian law, farmers were actually required to brew ale for the Yule celebration, with penalties for those who did not. Drinking julebryg today is a direct descendant of that midwinter feasting.

The Nordic countries, beer by beer

  • Denmark: julebryg, led by Tuborg and Carlsberg, plus a thriving craft scene.
  • Norway: juleøl, from easy lagers to rich, dark craft versions.
  • Sweden: julöl, often a maltier lager. (Do not confuse it with julmust, the Christmas soft drink.)
  • Finland: jouluolut, the Finnish take on the Christmas beer.
