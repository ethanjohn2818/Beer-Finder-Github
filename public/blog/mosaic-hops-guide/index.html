Mosaic hops explained: berries, tropics and a hint of pine

Published 28 August 2026 · by Katie

If Citra is craft beer's bright, obvious pop star, Mosaic is the moody, complex one. It's one of the most widely used aroma hops in the world, and it turns up in everything from crisp pale ales to thick, hazy double IPAs. But it's harder to pin down than most, because Mosaic doesn't taste of just one thing.

What Mosaic tastes like

Mosaic is famous for its range. In one beer it'll show tropical fruit, mango and passionfruit; in another it leans towards blueberry and dark berries; in a third you'll catch pine and a dank, herbal edge. That complexity is exactly why brewers love it: a single hop that brings several layers at once. It's the reason a Mosaic beer often tastes "deeper" or "moodier" than a straightforwardly citrussy one.

Mosaic vs Citra

They're relatives, Mosaic is actually a daughter of the same hop family as Citra, and they're often used together. The quick way to tell them apart: Citra is clean citrus and tropical fruit, while Mosaic adds berry, pine and a dank, almost savoury quality underneath. If a beer tastes juicy but also a bit mysterious, there's a good chance Mosaic is in there.

Where you'll find it

Mosaic is everywhere in modern craft. Salt's Vermont XPA is a lovely single-hop Mosaic pale that lets you learn the flavour on its own. Beyond that, most hazy IPAs on the shelf use it in a blend, often alongside Citra and Simcoe, so once you've spotted it, you'll start recognising it across dozens of beers.

Getting the most from it

Mosaic rewards drinking fresh. Like most aroma hops, its brightest fruity notes fade over time, so check the date on the can and drink hoppy beers young. Served a touch cooler than fridge-cold, its tropical side really opens up.
