Belgian Christmas ales: the Noël beers explained

Published 2 November 2026 · by Katie

If you want to taste Christmas in a glass, start in Belgium. The Belgians have a centuries-old genius for strong, characterful ales, and their Christmas beers, usually labelled "Noël" (French) or "Kerst" (Flemish), are some of the finest festive beers made anywhere.

What a Belgian Christmas ale tastes like

Most share a family resemblance: strong (typically 8% to 12%), dark amber to deep brown, and warmly spiced. Expect flavours of dark dried fruit (raisin, fig, date), caramel and brown sugar, a little chocolate, and a gentle lift of Christmas spice. They are smooth and warming rather than bitter, with the alcohol woven in rather than sticking out. A good one drinks like liquid Christmas pudding.

How they are made

Unlike German beer, Belgian brewing has no purity law, and that freedom is the whole point. Brewers use dark Belgian candi sugar to boost strength while keeping the body smooth, expressive Belgian yeast strains that throw off fruity and spicy notes, and often a literal pinch of spices. The result is complex and festive in a way few other beers manage. For the wider picture, see our guide to Belgian beer styles.

Names to look for

  • St Bernardus Christmas Ale (10%), a rich, dark quadrupel-style ale, widely loved and fairly easy to find.
  • Gouden Carolus Noël (10.5%), from Mechelen, deep and spiced with a warming finish.
  • Delirium Noël (10%), the Christmas version of the famous pink-elephant brewery.
  • Bush de Noël (12%), one of the strongest, amber and potent.
  • Stille Nacht from De Dolle (around 12%), a cult favourite that ages beautifully.
  • Corsendonk Christmas Ale and St Feuillien Cuvée de Noël, both classic, dependable Noels.

How to serve them

These are sipping beers. Serve cool, not cold, in a tulip or goblet glass that holds the aroma, and pour slowly to leave the yeast sediment in the bottle if you prefer a clearer glass. A single bottle shared after Christmas dinner, with cheese or pudding, is a lovely thing. Because they are strong, one is usually plenty.

Find a Belgian beer in the UK

True seasonal Noels come and go in the shops, but Belgian strong ales are around all year. See what festive beer is in stock on our Christmas beers page, and read our Christmas food pairing guide for what to drink them with.
