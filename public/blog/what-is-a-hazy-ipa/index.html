What is a hazy IPA? The juicy, cloudy beer explained

Published 27 August 2026 · by Katie

Walk down any beer aisle and you'll see them: cloudy, orange-juice-coloured cans promising "juicy", "hazy" and "New England" IPAs. It's the most talked-about style in modern craft beer, but what actually makes a hazy IPA hazy, and why does everyone love it?

Hazy, NEIPA, juicy, same thing?

Mostly, yes. "New England IPA" (NEIPA) is the original name, after the part of the US where the style was born around 2015. "Hazy IPA" and "juicy IPA" are the friendlier names that stuck. They all describe the same idea: an IPA that's soft, cloudy and packed with tropical-fruit hop flavour, with very little of the harsh bitterness of older IPAs.

Why is it cloudy?

The haze comes from a few things working together: lots of protein-rich grains like oats and wheat, a big dose of hops added after the boil (dry-hopping), and yeast strains that stay suspended rather than settling out. Brewers deliberately don't filter it clear. The cloudiness isn't a fault, it's the whole point, and it comes with the soft, almost creamy mouthfeel the style is loved for.

What it tastes like

Think tropical fruit smoothie with a gentle bitter finish. Mango, pineapple, passionfruit and citrus dominate, driven by hops like Citra, Mosaic, Galaxy and Nelson Sauvin. Because the bitterness is dialled right down, the fruit takes centre stage, which is exactly why hazy IPAs are such an easy on-ramp for people who thought they didn't like IPA.

Drink it fresh

This is the one style where the date really matters. Those bright, juicy hop aromas fade fast, a hazy IPA is at its best within a couple of months of canning and noticeably duller after six. Always check the date, store it cold, and don't hoard it.

Where to start

Supermarket shelves are full of them now. If you're new to the style, look for something in the 4–5% range described as "pale" or "session" before working up to the 6%+ double hazies.
