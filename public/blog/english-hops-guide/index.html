English hops explained: Fuggles, Goldings and the classic pint

Published 11 August 2026 · by Katie

Amid all the tropical fruit and pine of modern craft beer, it's easy to forget that Britain has its own proud hop tradition. English hops are the soul of a classic pint of bitter, best ale or golden ale, earthy, herbal and subtle where American hops are loud.

What English hops taste like

The two icons are Fuggles and East Kent Goldings. Fuggles is earthy, woody and mildly minty; Goldings is gently floral, honeyed and spicy. Together they give traditional ales their soft, rounded, moreish character, nothing jumps out and shouts, but everything sits in balance. Other classics include Challenger, Bramling Cross (with its unusual blackcurrant note) and Target.

A different philosophy

English hops were never bred to dominate a beer. In a traditional ale, the malt leads and the hops provide a herbal, balancing seasoning. That's a very different idea from a hazy IPA, where the hops are the whole point, and it's why a proper English bitter tastes so different from an American IPA even though both are "hoppy".

Where you'll find them

Breweries like Wye Valley, St Austell, Shepherd Neame and Timothy Taylor build their classic ales on English hops. Wye Valley's Butty Bach (Fuggles, Goldings and Bramling Cross) and St Austell's Tribute (Fuggle and Willamette) are great, widely available examples.
