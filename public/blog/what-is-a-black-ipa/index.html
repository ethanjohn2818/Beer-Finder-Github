What is a black IPA? Dark colour, hoppy soul

Published 21 July 2026 · by Katie

A black IPA looks like it should be a stout, it's jet black in the glass, but the first sip is pure hoppy IPA. It's one of craft beer's cleverest tricks, and a genuinely delicious one once you get past the visual confusion.

How it works

The style takes a hop-forward IPA and adds a small amount of debittered dark malt, roasted malt with the harsh, bitter, coffee notes stripped out. That gives the beer its black colour and a whisper of roast, without turning it into a stout. The hops still lead, so you get citrus and pine up front, with a subtle roasted, chocolatey edge underneath.

A style with many names

Black IPA has been called Cascadian Dark Ale, American Black Ale and India Black Ale, brewers never quite agreed on a name, partly because "black" and "pale" in one title is a contradiction. Whatever it's called, the idea is the same: IPA flavour, stout looks.

Who it's for

Black IPA is a brilliant bridge beer. If you love IPAs but fancy something with a bit more depth and a roasty backbone, or you like dark beer but want more hop punch, this is the style that scratches both itches at once.
