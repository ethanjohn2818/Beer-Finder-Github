How to store beer properly (and why it matters)

Published 17 July 2026 · by Katie

You've spent good money on great beer, don't let it go off in a warm cupboard. Storing beer well is simple, but a few easy mistakes can quietly ruin a good can. Here's how to keep your beer tasting the way the brewer intended.

Cold is king

Heat is beer's biggest enemy. Warm storage speeds up the chemical reactions that make a beer taste stale and cardboardy, and it destroys delicate hop aromas fastest of all. Keep beer cold, or at least cool and stable, a fridge is ideal, especially for anything hoppy. A hazy IPA left warm for a few weeks is a shadow of a chilled one.

Keep it in the dark

Light, especially sunlight and fluorescent light, reacts with hop compounds to create a notoriously skunky smell in minutes. It's why beer comes in brown glass or cans. Store bottles in the dark, and don't leave beer sitting in a sunny window.

Upright, not on its side

Unlike wine, beer is best stored upright. It reduces the surface area exposed to the air in the neck, and for bottle-conditioned beers it keeps the yeast settled neatly at the bottom, so you can pour clear.

Drink hoppy beer fresh, age strong beer

Most beer is meant to be drunk fresh, hoppy styles within a couple of months. The exceptions are strong, malty beers like barley wines and imperial stouts, which can improve over years if cellared cool and dark. Always check the date and drink hop-forward beers young.
