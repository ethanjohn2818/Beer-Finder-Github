Spaten: the Munich brewery that shaped the modern lager

Published 30 September 2026 · by Katie

Spaten may not be the loudest name at Oktoberfest, but it might be the most important brewery in the history of lager. The spade on its label (Spaten means "spade" in German) marks a brewery that helped invent the modern beer you drink today.

It is one of the six Munich breweries allowed to pour at Oktoberfest, alongside Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten.

Centuries of history

Records of the Spaten brewery in Munich go back to 1397, but its golden era came in the 1800s under the Sedlmayr family. Gabriel Sedlmayr the Younger was one of the brewers who, with new science and cooling technology, perfected cold-fermented lager and helped spread it across the world. If you have ever drunk a crisp golden lager anywhere on earth, you owe a little of it to Spaten.

The original Oktoberfest beer

Spaten also has a strong claim on Oktoberfest itself. It was a Sedlmayr who helped popularise the amber Märzen that became the festival's signature beer in the 19th century, and Spaten was central to the later move toward the paler Festbier poured today. At the festival, Spaten runs the Ochsenbraterei, the tent famous for roasting whole oxen on a spit.

What to try

  • Spaten Münchner Hell, a textbook Munich Helles: pale, soft, clean and gently malty.
  • Spaten Oktoberfestbier, the strong golden festival lager, when you can find it in season.

Today Spaten is part of the global AB InBev group, but its Munich beers are still brewed in the city to the German purity law. For more on the styles it helped create, see our craft lager guide.

Find Spaten in the UK

Spaten's Helles turns up in UK supermarkets and is a genuinely authentic taste of Munich, brewed by an actual Oktoberfest brewery. See where to buy it on the Oktoberfest beers page. Prost!
