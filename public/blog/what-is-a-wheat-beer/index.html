Wheat beer explained: witbier, hefeweizen and the summer classic

Published 27 July 2026 · by Katie

Cloudy, soft and famously refreshing, wheat beer is one of the great summer styles. But "wheat beer" actually covers two quite different traditions, the German hefeweizen and the Belgian witbier, and knowing the difference helps you pick the right one.

Why wheat?

Brewing with a large proportion of wheat alongside barley gives these beers their pale, hazy appearance and a soft, fluffy mouthfeel. But the real character comes from the yeast, which produces those distinctive fruity and spicy aromas. Hops take a back seat entirely.

Hefeweizen (German)

The Bavarian classic. A hefeweizen is defined by its yeast, which throws off unmistakable notes of banana and clove. It's cloudy, refreshing and usually around 5%, with almost no hop bitterness. "Hefe" means yeast, "weizen" means wheat, it's literally "yeast wheat".

Witbier (Belgian)

The Belgian take is spiced rather than banana-forward. A witbier ("white beer") is brewed with orange peel and coriander, giving a zesty, citrussy, gently spicy character. It's light, tangy and one of the easiest-drinking styles around, think of a classic like Hoegaarden.

Serving tip

Serve wheat beers cold and cloudy, many come with instructions to rouse the yeast at the bottom of the bottle. A slice of orange suits a witbier; a hefeweizen needs nothing but a tall glass.
