Simcoe hops: pine, grapefruit and the West Coast sound

Published 18 August 2026 · by Katie

If Citra and Galaxy are the sound of the juicy, hazy era, Simcoe is the sound of the West Coast IPA that came before it. It's a versatile American hop that brings both bitterness and aroma, and it's responsible for that classic piney, resinous bite so many long-time IPA drinkers love.

What Simcoe tastes like

Think pine and resin first, then grapefruit and a dank, earthy, almost herbal edge. It's greener and more savoury than the outright tropical hops, and it gives a beer real backbone. In a West Coast IPA it delivers that satisfying, mouth-coating bitterness; used lightly it adds depth and a whiff of the forest.

Where it comes from

Simcoe was released in 2000 by Yakima Chief Ranches in the US. It was one of the hops that helped define American craft beer's hoppy golden age, and it remains a favourite for its dual role: a clean bittering hop and a characterful aroma hop in one.

How it's used

Simcoe is a team player as often as a soloist. It's frequently blended with Citra and Mosaic to add a piney counterweight to their fruitiness — the pine-and-tropical combination is one of the most reliable in modern brewing. In West Coast IPAs it often does the heavy lifting for both bitterness and aroma.
