Christmas beer and food pairing: what to drink with dinner

Published 26 October 2026 · by Katie

Here is a festive hot take: beer beats wine at the Christmas table. Christmas food is big, rich and varied, roast meats, heavy sides, powerful cheeses, sweet puddings, and beer's range of flavours and its refreshing carbonation handle all of it better than a single bottle of wine ever could. Here is what to pour, course by course.

Before dinner: nibbles and smoked salmon

Start light and crisp. A good lager, a pilsner or a dry saison cuts through smoked salmon, canapés and salty snacks and resets the palate. Save the big beers for later. See our craft lager guide for the crisp end of the spectrum.

The main event: turkey and all the trimmings

Roast turkey is delicate, but the trimmings are rich, so you want a beer with some malt depth that will not flatten the bird. Great choices:

  • An amber ale or winter warmer, malty enough for the gravy and pigs in blankets.
  • A Belgian dubbel or Christmas ale, whose dark-fruit and spice notes love roast dinner.
  • A Märzen or amber lager if you want something cleaner and less filling.

For goose or a rich beef roast, go bolder still, a strong dark ale stands right up to it.

The cheeseboard

This is where beer really wins. The classic: Stilton or other blue cheese with a strong, sweet beer, a barley wine, an imperial stout or a Belgian quad. The sweetness and the salty, funky cheese set each other off perfectly. Hard, nutty cheeses love a malty amber ale; creamy cheeses like something crisp.

Pudding and mince pies

Match sweet with sweet, and rich with rich. A Christmas stout or pastry stout with Christmas pudding, mince pies or chocolate yule log is a showstopper, chocolate and dried fruit on both sides of the table. Our Christmas stouts guide has the details.

A simple rule of thumb

If you remember nothing else: match the weight of the beer to the weight of the food (light with light, rich with rich), and use fizz and roast/bitter flavours to cut through fat. Do that and you cannot go far wrong.

Build your Christmas line-up

You do not need a different beer for every course, two or three well-chosen bottles will carry the whole day. See what festive beer is in stock on our Christmas beers page, and if you are buying for a beer lover, our page has glassware and gift sets to finish the present off.
