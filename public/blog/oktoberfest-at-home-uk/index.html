How to celebrate Oktoberfest at home in the UK

Published 5 October 2026 · by Katie

Not everyone can get to Munich in September, but the good news is that a proper Oktoberfest is surprisingly easy to recreate at home. You need the right beer, a few classic snacks, and a bit of oompah. Here is how to do it without a plane ticket.

1. Start with the beer

This is the one thing worth getting right. True imported Oktoberfestbier is seasonal and hard to find, but the festival's own breweries sell Munich lagers and wheat beers in UK supermarkets all year round, and they are exactly the right flavour for the occasion. Look for:

  • Munich Helles, the pale, soft, malty lager that is the everyday drink of Bavaria. A Spaten Helles, from one of the six Oktoberfest breweries, is about as authentic as it gets on a British shelf.
  • Weissbier, cloudy Bavarian wheat beer full of banana and clove character. Paulaner, Erdinger and Franziskaner all sell theirs here.
  • A Radler, lager cut with cloudy lemonade, if you want something lighter for the daytime.

We keep a running list of the Bavarian and festival-style beers you can actually buy in the UK on our Oktoberfest beers page, with the cheapest shop for each. If you are new to the style, our guide to Oktoberfestbier and Märzen explains what you are tasting, and our wheat beer guide covers the Weissbier side.

2. Serve it properly

Serve your lager cold but not ice-cold, around 7 to 8 degrees, so the malt flavour actually comes through. If you have a stein or a tall Weissbier glass, use it; the shape genuinely helps. No stein? A straight pint glass is absolutely fine. Pour with a bit of height to get a good foamy head, which is part of the experience.

3. The food

You do not need to roast a pork knuckle to do this well. A few easy wins:

  • Soft pretzels, warm, with proper German mustard.
  • Good bratwurst or any decent sausage, with sauerkraut if you like it.
  • Roast chicken, the festival's single most popular dish, so a supermarket rotisserie bird is spot on.
  • A board of cheese and cold meats for grazing.

4. The atmosphere

Put an oompah or Bavarian brass playlist on, the streaming services all have one. Learn the toast: raise your glass, call out "Prost!", clink, and look each other in the eye. Bunting in blue and white, Bavaria's colours, costs almost nothing and instantly sets the scene. Lederhosen and Dirndl are optional but very much in the spirit.

5. Keep it sociable

Oktoberfest beer is strong, usually around 6%, and Bavarian hospitality is generous. The whole idea of the festival is Gemütlichkeit, good company and a warm, easy atmosphere, not getting through as much as possible. Have water and plenty of food on the table, and enjoy the afternoon. Please drink responsibly.
