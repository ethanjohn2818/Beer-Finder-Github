Sour beer for beginners: tart, tangy and surprisingly moreish

Published 5 August 2026 · by Katie

The first sip of a sour beer can be a shock, beer isn't supposed to taste tart, is it? But sour beer is one of the oldest and most rewarding corners of brewing, and for many people it becomes a genuine obsession. Here's how to approach it.

Why is it sour?

Normal beer is soured by accident and considered a fault; sour beer makes it happen on purpose. Brewers introduce specific bacteria and wild yeasts, mainly Lactobacillus, that produce lactic acid, giving a clean, tangy tartness, like a squeeze of lemon or a spoonful of yoghurt. It's a controlled, deliberate sourness, not spoilage.

The main styles

  • Gose, a light, refreshing German-style sour with a pinch of salt and coriander.
  • Berliner Weisse, low-alcohol, sharply tart and thirst-quenching, often served with fruit.
  • Fruited / smoothie sours, modern sours loaded with real fruit (and sometimes lactose) for a thick, jammy, almost dessert-like drink.
  • Lambic & wild ales, complex, funky, barrel-aged Belgian sours; the deep end of the style.

How to start

Ease in with a fruited sour, the fruit softens the tartness and makes it approachable, like a fizzy, grown-up fruit squash. From there you can work towards the drier, funkier styles. Serve them cold; the tartness makes them incredibly refreshing on a hot day.
