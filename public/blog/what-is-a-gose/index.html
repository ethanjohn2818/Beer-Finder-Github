What is a gose? The salty sour you didn't know you'd love

Published 3 August 2026 · by Katie

Pronounced "GOH-zuh", this is one of beer's great comeback stories: a centuries-old German style that nearly went extinct, only to be rediscovered by craft brewers and turned into a summer staple. And yes, it has salt in it.

What makes a gose a gose

A gose is a tart, lightly sour wheat beer brewed with two unusual ingredients: a pinch of salt and a dash of coriander. The sourness is gentle and lemony, the salt is subtle and rounds everything out, and the coriander adds a soft spice. It's usually low in alcohol (around 4%) and wonderfully refreshing.

Why the salt?

Gose originated in the German town of Goslar, whose local water was naturally salty. Brewers leaned into it, and the slight salinity became part of the style's identity. It sounds strange, but it works the same way a pinch of salt lifts a meal, it makes the beer taste rounder and more moreish, and it's why a good gose is so easy to drink.

The modern twist

Craft brewers love a gose because it's a brilliant canvas for fruit. Modern versions come loaded with raspberry, mango, passionfruit and more, the tartness and salt playing beautifully against the sweetness. If you like the idea of a fizzy, fruity, slightly savoury cooler, this is your style.
