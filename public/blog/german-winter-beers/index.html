German winter beers: Winterbock, Doppelbock and Festbier

Published 31 October 2026 · by Katie

Germany does not really do spiced Christmas ale. Its answer to winter is the bock: a strong, smooth, deeply malty lager brewed to keep you warm when the temperature drops. If you like your festive beer clean and malty rather than sweet and spiced, this is your tradition.

Meet the bock family

"Bock" means a strong lager, and it comes in several strengths:

  • Bock, the base: a strong (6.5% or so) malty lager, amber to brown, rich in bready, toasty malt.
  • Doppelbock, "double bock", stronger still (7% to 9%+) and even richer, with notes of dark bread, caramel, dried fruit and sometimes chocolate. Their names traditionally end in "-ator" (Salvator, Celebrator, Optimator).
  • Winterbock and Weihnachtsbock (Christmas bock), seasonal versions brewed specially for the cold months.
  • Eisbock, the extreme one: a Doppelbock partly frozen to concentrate it, reaching well into double figures.

Monks, Lent and "liquid bread"

The Doppelbock has a wonderful origin story. It was brewed by monks in Munich, the Paulaner friars, to sustain them through Lenten fasting. Rich and full of calories, it was their "liquid bread". The original was called Salvator, and that is why so many Doppelbocks still carry an "-ator" name in tribute. There is more on Munich's brewing monks in our craft lager guide.

The strongest Christmas beer of all

If you want to taste the extreme end, look for Samichlaus. Brewed only once a year on Saint Nicholas Day (6 December) and then aged for months, it reaches around 14%, making it one of the strongest lagers in the world. It is a true sipping beer, best in a small glass after dinner.

How to serve a bock

Serve it cool but not ice-cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, so the malt richness comes through. A bock is smooth and warming rather than bitter, which makes it a brilliant match for roast meats, hard cheeses and anything off the Christmas table.

Find a German beer in the UK

Seasonal bocks are harder to find in British supermarkets than everyday German lagers, but they do appear, and the style is worth seeking out. See what festive beer is in stock on our Christmas beers page.
