What temperature should you serve beer?

Published 15 July 2026 · by Katie

We're trained to drink beer ice-cold, but that habit is doing a lot of good beer a disservice. Temperature dramatically changes how a beer tastes, and serving it a few degrees warmer often unlocks flavours you didn't know were there.

Why temperature matters

Cold suppresses flavour and aroma. That's great for a mass-market lager brewed to taste of very little, you want it numbingly cold. But for a flavourful craft beer, serving it too cold hides the very hop and malt character you paid for. Let it warm slightly and the aromas open up.

A rough guide by style

  • Lagers, pilsners, wheat beers, sours, serve cold, around 4–7°C. Crispness is the point.
  • Pale ales and IPAs, a touch warmer, around 7–10°C, so the hop aroma sings.
  • Amber and red ales, porters, around 10–12°C to bring out the malt.
  • Stouts, barley wines, strong Belgians, cellar temperature, 12–14°C. Cold mutes their rich complexity.

The easy trick

You don't need a thermometer. Take a flavourful beer out of the fridge and let it sit for 5–10 minutes before drinking, then keep sipping as it warms, you'll notice new flavours appear. Fridge-cold is a starting point, not the destination.

The glass helps too

Pouring into a glass rather than drinking from the can also releases aroma and lets the beer warm gently. For more, see our beer glassware guide.
