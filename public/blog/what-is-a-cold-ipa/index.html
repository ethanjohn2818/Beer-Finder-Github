What is a cold IPA? The newest twist on the IPA

Published 19 July 2026 · by Katie

Just when you thought the IPA couldn't be reinvented again, along came the cold IPA — one of the newest styles to hit the shelves. Despite the name, it's not about temperature; it's about a clever mash-up of ale and lager brewing.

What makes it "cold"

A cold IPA takes a hoppy IPA recipe but brews it using lager yeast fermented cool, and often adds a portion of corn or rice to the grain. The result is a beer with all the big hop aroma of an IPA but a cleaner, crisper, drier finish — the hops stand out even more sharply because there's less malt sweetness getting in the way.

How it tastes

Think of it as a West Coast IPA turned up for clarity and crispness: intense citrus and pine hops over a lean, dry, almost lager-like body. It's punchy but incredibly easy to drink — dangerously so, given they're often strong.

Cold IPA vs the rest

Compared to a hazy IPA it's the polar opposite — clear, dry and crisp rather than soft and juicy. Compared to a West Coast IPA it's even cleaner and more lager-like. And compared to an actual IPL (India Pale Lager), it's hoppier and usually stronger. It's the hop-lover's crisp beer.
