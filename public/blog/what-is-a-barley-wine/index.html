What is a barley wine? Beer's biggest, boldest style

Published 23 July 2026 · by Katie

Despite the name, there's no wine in a barley wine, the name is a nod to its strength, which rivals a glass of wine, and to the fact that it's meant to be sipped slowly rather than gulped. It's one of beer's oldest and grandest styles.

What defines a barley wine

A barley wine is a very strong ale, typically 8–12% ABV, brewed with a huge amount of malt for an intensely rich, sweet, complex character. Expect toffee, dried fruit, caramel, marmalade and a warming, boozy finish. It's a big, contemplative beer, the sort you pour into a small glass and nurse by the fire.

English vs American

English barley wines lean towards rich malt, dried fruit and a gentle, earthy hop balance. American versions crank up the hops for a bolder, more bitter, citrussy counterpoint to all that sweetness. Both reward slow drinking.

It ages like wine

Here's the clever part: unlike a hazy IPA, a barley wine improves with age. Its high strength acts as a preservative, and over months or years the flavours mellow and deepen into sherry-like, port-like complexity. Many people buy two, one to drink now, one to cellar.

Find a barley wine

On MyBeerFinder you can find strong ales and barley wines and compare prices. Robinsons Old Tom is a classic English example to look out for. Serve it cool, not cold, in a small glass.
