American Christmas beers: Anchor, Celebration and friends

Published 29 October 2026 · by Katie

Modern craft beer was born in America, and so was the modern Christmas beer. When US craft brewers got going in the 1970s and 80s, two of them created festive beers so influential that they more or less defined the whole category. Both are still brewed today, and both are worth seeking out.

Anchor Christmas Ale: a different beer every year

Anchor Brewing of San Francisco released its first Christmas Ale, called "Our Special Ale", in 1975. Its party trick is that the recipe changes every single year, and so does the hand-drawn tree on the label (a different tree each time, symbolising the turning of the year). It is a spiced dark ale, secretive about exactly which spices, and fans collect it year to year to compare. Sadly Anchor closed in 2023, which has made surviving bottles something of a collector's item, but its influence on every spiced holiday ale that followed is enormous.

Sierra Nevada Celebration: the hoppy winter warmer

Sierra Nevada took the Christmas beer in the opposite direction. Its Celebration Ale, first brewed in 1981, is a fresh-hop winter IPA: deep amber, malty and warming, but piled with piney, citrussy American hops using the first hops of the autumn harvest. No spices at all, just big hop flavour. It proved that a Christmas beer did not have to be sweet and spiced, and it remains one of the most eagerly awaited seasonals in the US every year.

The two American traditions

Between them, Anchor and Sierra Nevada gave American craft beer two festive templates that brewers still follow:

  • The spiced holiday ale, dark, malty and seasoned with cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg and orange, like Great Lakes Christmas Ale with its honey and spice.
  • The hoppy winter warmer, strong and malty but showcasing hops rather than spice.

If you want to understand the hop-forward side, our West Coast IPA guide explains the American hop character that makes a beer like Celebration sing.
