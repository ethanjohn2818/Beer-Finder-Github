British Christmas ales and winter warmers

Published 1 November 2026 · by Katie

Britain has a long, cosy relationship with winter beer. Long before craft brewers started adding gingerbread to stouts, British breweries were rolling out strong, malty winter ales to warm the darkest months. These are the beers of the British Christmas: rich, comforting and built for a fireside.

The British style

A British winter ale or Christmas ale tends to be amber to dark brown, strong by British standards (often 5% to 7%, sometimes more), and led by malt rather than hops. Expect caramel, toffee, nuts, dried fruit and biscuit, with just enough bitterness to keep it balanced. Some are spiced for the season; many are not, letting the malt and a touch of alcohol do the warming. For the broader family, see our guide to winter warmers.

Classics to look out for

  • Robinsons Old Tom, a rich, dark strong ale (around 8.5%) that is a proper winter sipper, and often on UK shelves.
  • Wychwood Bah Humbug, a cinnamon-spiced Christmas ale, and King Goblin, a stronger seasonal version of Hobgoblin.
  • Shepherd Neame Christmas Ale, a strong, warming amber ale from Britain's oldest brewer.
  • Fuller's Old Winter Ale, a classic malty seasonal, and the much bigger Vintage Ale for serious sipping.
  • Theakston Old Peculier and Harvey's Christmas Ale, old-school old ales that suit the season perfectly.

Cask, bottle and can

Traditionally these beers shine on cask in a warm pub, served cool rather than cold and softly carbonated. At home, bottles and cans do the job nicely. Whatever the format, do not over-chill them: a British winter ale wants to be a little cool of room temperature so the malt sweetness and dried-fruit character open up.

A note on strength

Winter ales are stronger than your average session pint, so they reward slow drinking. One after dinner, with cheese and biscuits or a slice of Christmas cake, is the British winter in a glass. Please enjoy them responsibly.
