Sabro hops: coconut, tangerine and something different

Published 12 August 2026 · by Katie

Every so often a hop comes along that tastes like nothing else. Sabro is that hop. Its coconut-and-citrus character is so unusual that drinkers tend to either love it or find it baffling, but either way, it's unforgettable.

What Sabro tastes like

The headline note is coconut, joined by tangerine, tropical fruit and a hint of cedar and stone fruit. It has an almost creamy, piña-colada quality that's completely distinct from other hops. Used carefully it adds a luscious tropical richness; overused it can tip into a soapy, sun-cream character, which is why brewers treat it with respect.

Where it comes from

Sabro is a relatively new American hop, released in 2018, bred from a wild hop with an unusually complex lineage. That unusual parentage is exactly where its one-of-a-kind flavour comes from.

How it's used

Because Sabro is so distinctive, it's usually used as an accent alongside cleaner hops like Citra and Amarillo, which rein in its wilder edges. It's brilliant in hazy IPAs and pale ales where a little tropical, coconutty lift lifts the whole beer.
