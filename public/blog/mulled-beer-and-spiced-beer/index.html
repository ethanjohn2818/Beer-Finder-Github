Mulled beer and spiced beer: a cosy Christmas how-to

Published 27 October 2026 · by Katie

Everyone knows mulled wine. Far fewer people know that warm, spiced beer has just as long a history, and in Britain it might be the older tradition of the two. If you have a cold evening and a bottle of dark ale, you can make something wonderfully festive in about ten minutes.

A very old British tradition

Warming and spicing ale goes back centuries in Britain. Victorian Christmases featured hot spiced-ale drinks with names like "Smoking Bishop" (immortalised in A Christmas Carol) and the "wassail" bowl, a hot, spiced, sometimes ale-based punch shared from door to door while singing. Germany has its own version, Glühbier, the beery cousin of Glühwein. The point was always the same: a warm, spiced drink to share in the cold and the dark.

How to mull beer at home

The method is simple, and the one rule is: do not boil it. Boiling drives off the alcohol and the delicate aromatics, and can turn the beer bitter.

  • Pick the right beer. Use something dark and malty: a winter warmer, a mild, a porter or a sweet stout. Avoid hoppy IPAs, which turn harsh when warmed.
  • Warm it gently. Pour into a saucepan and heat slowly to steaming, never simmering, around 60 to 70 degrees.
  • Add the spices. A cinnamon stick, a few cloves, a star anise, a strip of orange peel, and a spoonful of honey or brown sugar to taste.
  • Let it infuse for five to ten minutes off a rolling heat, then strain into a mug.
  • Optional: a small splash of brandy or dark rum makes it properly festive.

Serve it in a heatproof glass or mug with a fresh strip of orange peel. It is the perfect thing after a cold walk, or for handing round while the presents are opened.

Or buy it ready-spiced

If you would rather not do the work, plenty of brewers make spiced winter ales and festive beers that deliver the same cosy flavours cold, straight from the can or bottle. Our winter warmer guide explains the style, and many of them mull beautifully too.

Find a beer to mull
